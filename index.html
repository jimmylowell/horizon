Sunset is the moment the top edge of the sun drops out of sight, the definition published times use (Wikipedia, Sunset: "the moment the upper limb of the Sun disappears below the horizon"); sunrise is the top edge appearing. The air bends sunlight near the horizon, so at that moment the disc is really about one diameter below it. Behind a ridge, the time is the moment the top edge meets the ridge line, worked out the same way, so the minutes a spot gains or loses are the terrain alone. The moon is timed the same way.

In town, buildings within 600 m of the pin, toward the event, are counted too, from OpenStreetMap footprints: a building's tagged height, else 3 m a storey, else one storey of 4 m (most footprints carry no height, so that last one is a guess).

In Salida the footprints are replaced by measured heights: the USGS lidar flight CO_SanLuisJuanMiguel_2020_D20, the highest laser return in each metre-square, less the USGS 1 m bare-ground model from the same flight, marched 1.5 km from the pin. The USGS catalog gives the collection as 21 September to 19 October 2019; the points' own time stamps over downtown read 21 to 29 September 2020. Which year is right is unknown here. What it cannot know: anything built, cut down or grown since the flight; trees count as solid, leaves or not, and a gap you could see through between branches reads as a wall; and the flight labels nothing above the ground as roof or tree, so neither can this page. Wires are left out. Floors and balconies are not recorded anywhere here, so the view is always from 2 m above the street.

The strip under the reading is the skyline from the pin, 25° either side of the event: the ridge, and in Salida the roofs and trees in green, with the sun's or moon's path over it, a dot every ten minutes, and the time it meets the skyline.

Away looks at the 60° of sky straight opposite the sun. The Belt of Venus is a pink band "roughly 10–20° above the horizon" there during twilight, over the dark band of Earth's shadow (Wikipedia, Belt of Venus), so a spot is graded by how low its horizon is on that side.
