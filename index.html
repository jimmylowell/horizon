Sunset is the moment the top edge of the sun drops out of sight, the definition published times use (Wikipedia, Sunset: "the moment the upper limb of the Sun disappears below the horizon"); sunrise is the top edge appearing. The air bends sunlight near the horizon, so at that moment the disc is really about one diameter below it. Behind a ridge, the time is the moment the top edge meets the ridge line, worked out the same way, so the minutes a spot gains or loses are the terrain alone. The moon is timed the same way.

In Salida roofs and trees are measured: the USGS lidar flight CO_SanLuisJuanMiguel_2020_D20, the highest laser return in each metre-square, less the USGS 1 m bare-ground model from the same flight, marched 1.5 km from the pin. The USGS catalog gives the collection as 21 September to 19 October 2019; the points' own time stamps over downtown read 21 to 29 September 2020. Which year is right is unknown here. What it cannot know: anything built, cut down or grown since the flight; trees count as solid, leaves or not, and a gap you could see through between branches reads as a wall; and the flight labels nothing above the ground as roof or tree, so neither can this page. Wires are left out. Floors and balconies are not recorded anywhere here; the eye height in Setup is height above the ground at the pin. Outside Salida no roofs or trees are counted.

The strip is the skyline from the pin all the way round: the ridge, and in Salida the roofs and trees in green, with the sun's or moon's path over it, a dot every ten minutes, and the time it meets the skyline. Heights are stretched to be readable. Toward the event the ridge is read 120 km out, the rest of the circle 30 km. Tap the skyline and the page names what is there from OpenStreetMap: the building whose outline holds that lidar point, or the named peak or ridge; where no outline holds it, it says "a tree or something unmapped", since the flight cannot tell.

The colours on the map are how late the sun stays (or how early it clears) at each point round the pin, from the same eye height: blue later, red sooner, against the middle of the area shown. In Salida 400 m round the pin over the lidar (points inside a roof or a tree at that eye height are left out), elsewhere 3 km of terrain.
